### Adaptive vs fixed, Llama 3.1 8B, 32,768-token context, three receivers

Seconds until each receiver holds the cache; each receiver recomputes on one H100-equivalent. The ~4× codec is lossy (CacheGen reports no more than 2% accuracy loss), so class T; its encode and decode time is left out, which flatters it. Default serving kernels are class N. ## 7. The ceiling on any byte-saving scheme (Amdahl)

If a fraction f of the time is spent moving bytes and the bytes shrink k-fold, speedup = 1 / ((1 - f) + f / k).

| f (byte-bound share of the time) | k = 2 | k = 10 | k = 100 | k → ∞ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 1.05× | 1.10× | 1.11× | 1.11× |
| 30% | 1.18× | 1.37× | 1.42× | 1.43× |
| 50% | 1.33× | 1.82× | 1.98× | 2.00× |
| 90% | 1.82× | 5.26× | 9.17× | 10.00× |
| 99% | 1.98× | 9.17× | 50.25× | 100.00× |

Back-of-envelope from Google Search (23% fewer HTML bytes, 1.7% better LCP overall, up to 9% on high-latency networks): if LCP scaled linearly in its HTML-byte-sensitive part, that part was about 7% of LCP overall and up to about 39% on high-latency networks (where saved slow-start round trips likely account for much of it).
